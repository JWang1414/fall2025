### Time-Reversal Symmetry
Recall the state and operator we defined as:
$$
\ket{\tilde{\psi}} = \hat{\Theta} \ket{\psi}
$$
If we revert the time of something, we expect it to go back to its previous location, with the same momentum.
$$
\hat{\Theta}^{-1} \ket{\tilde{\psi}(t)} = \ket{\psi(-t)}
$$
What happens under a "short time limit"?
$$
\hat{\Theta}^{-1} \left( 1- \frac{i\hat{H}\epsilon}{\hbar} \right)\hat{\Theta} \ket{\psi} = \left( 1+\frac{i\hat{H}\epsilon}{\hbar} \right)\ket{\psi}
$$
Where we are using the Taylor series for the time propagation operator
$$
e^{ -i\hat{H}t/\hbar } \approx 1 - \frac{i\hat{H}\epsilon}{\hbar}
$$
From the above, we gain the equivalence,
$$
-\frac{i\epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{\Theta}^{-1} \hat{H} \hat{\Theta} = \frac{i\epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{H} \implies \hat{\Theta}^{-1}\hat{H}\hat{\Theta} = \hat{H} \implies \hat{\Theta} \hat{H} = - \hat{H}\hat{\Theta}
$$
What happens if both of these operate on some state?
$$
\Theta H \ket{\psi_{n}}  = E_{n} \Theta \ket{\psi_{n}}  = -H\Theta \ket{\psi_{n}}
$$
And this equivalence disturbingly tells us that
$$
H \Theta \ket{\psi_{n}} = -E_{n} \Theta \ket{\psi_{n}}
$$
Which is a state with negative energy. This arises because we neglected to entertain the fact that, under symmetries, it is possible for $\Theta$ to be an anti-linear operator. Specifically an anti-unitary operator.

This means that, for some complex constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$ we have,
$$
\Theta (\alpha \ket{a} + \beta \ket{b} )= \alpha^* \Theta \ket{a}  + \beta^* \Theta \ket{b}
$$
And so, in actuality, we have the equivalence,
$$
\frac{i\epsilon}{\hbar} \Theta ^{-1} H \Theta = \frac{i\epsilon}{\hbar}H
$$
Which solves our issues and familiarly tells us that $\hat{\Theta}$ commutes with $\hat{H}$.

Note that we have,
$$
\Theta ^{-1} \hat{x} \Theta = \hat{x}
$$
$$
\Theta ^{-1}\hat{p} \Theta = -\hat{p}
$$
Which makes sense, and also arises because we assume $\hat{\Theta}$ is an anti-unitary operator.
$$
\Theta ^{-1} \vec{L} \Theta = \Theta ^{-1} (\vec{r} \times \vec{p}) \Theta = -\vec{L}
$$
Recall the commutator for $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ is,
$$
[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar
$$
We expect nothing to change about this under time reversal.
$$
\Theta ^{-1} (\hat{x}\hat{p} - \hat{p}\hat{x}) \Theta = -\hat{x}\hat{p} + \hat{p}\hat{x} = -[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]
$$
$$
\Theta ^{-1} (i\hbar) \Theta = -i\hbar
$$
And so we recover that $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]=i\hbar$. You can complete something similar with angular momentum.
$$
[L_{a}, L_{b}] = i\hbar \epsilon_{abc} L_{c}
$$
Will remain invariant under time reversal as well.

Since spin is generated by angular momentum, we expect it to behave similarly,
$$
\Theta ^{-1} \vec{S} \Theta = -\vec{S}
$$
### Spin-1/2
We might naively expect that, under time reversal, we have,
$$
\Theta \ket{+}  = \ket{-}
$$
$$
\Theta \ket{-} = \ket{+}
$$
So an arbitrary spin-1/2 state might be,
$$
\Theta \left( \alpha \ket{+} + \beta \ket{-}  \right) = \alpha^* \ket{-} + \beta^* \ket{+}
$$
The expectation value for some arbitrary state from the $S_{z}$ operator might be:
$$
\bra{\psi} S_{z} \ket{\psi} = \frac{\hbar}{2} \left( |\alpha|^{2} - |\beta|^{2} \right)
$$
Which you can work out from the Pauli matrix representation for $S_{z}= (\hbar /2)\sigma_{z}$ and using arbitrary vectors $[\alpha, \beta]$. For some time reversed state we have,
$$
\bra{\tilde{\psi}} S_{z} \ket{\tilde{\psi}} = \frac{\hbar}{2} (|\beta|^{2} - |\alpha|^{2})
$$
And furthermore,
$$
\bra{\psi} S_{x} \ket{\psi} = \frac{\hbar}{2} (\alpha^* \beta + \beta^* \alpha)
$$
$$
\bra{\tilde{\psi}} S_{x} \ket{\tilde{\psi}}  = \frac{\hbar}{2} (\alpha^* \beta + \beta^* \alpha)
$$
Which, unlike the other state, has no changed sign. We expect this to change sign, telling us that our original assumption was incorrect. The behaviour of time reversal is actually,
$$
\Theta \ket{+}  = \ket{-}
$$
$$
\Theta \ket{-}  = - \ket{+}
$$
And, in this case, all the expectation values work out.

Based on this analysis, in the spin-1/2 basis, we can try to find the representation of $\Theta$
$$
\Theta = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & -1 \\
1 & 0
\end{bmatrix} K = -i \sigma_{y} K
$$
Where $K$ is some operator who's job is to take complex conjugates. Therefore,
$$
\Theta ^{-1} S_{x} \Theta = (i\sigma_{y} K) \left( \frac{\hbar}{2} \sigma_{x} \right) (-i\sigma_{y}K)
$$
We can bring the first complex conjugate through these operators. At the end, we have $K^{2}=1$ and so,
$$
\Theta ^{-1} S_{x} \Theta = \frac{\hbar}{2} \sigma_{y} \sigma_{x} \sigma_{y}
$$
Recall the properties of Pauli matrices:
$$
\sigma_{x} \sigma_{y} = - \sigma_{y} \sigma_{x}
$$
$$
\sigma^{2}_{y}= \hat{1}
$$
So the above becomes,
$$
\frac{\hbar}{2} \sigma_{y} \sigma_{x} \sigma_{y} = - \frac{\hbar}{2} \sigma_{x} =-S_{x}
$$
Which is the answer we expect.
