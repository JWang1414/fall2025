A conversation among Robin Wall Kimmerer, Peter Victor, Prof. Simon Appolloni and you on economics and the state of the environment today.

Topic:
- Gift economies, degrowth, and consumption
	- The American culture is centred around consumption and growth, decreasing happiness, and eating up resources
	- Artificial scarcity
- Market capitalism is based on scarcities and material wealth. We need to hoard and build up resources in an unstable manner just for our societies to function
- Gift economies don't just build gratitude, but also reduce resource consumption
	- This sort of "spreading the wealth" is likely to impact the genuine progress meter
- I think I'll focus on the market economy + capitalism. Raj Patel and Jason Moore in *Cheap Nature*, for example, may believe that capitalism will not survive a degrowth. If not because it isn't compatible, than because it is either not viable or efficient under these circumstances

# Convey
What do these authors say about how the economy is connected to the current state of the environment _and_ how might the economy contribute to solutions?

It is worth noting that all the following authors acknowledge that capitalism is operated by the people at the top. That is, the overarching capitalist system cannot be altered without convincing policymakers/lawmakers and the most wealthy businessmen.

Kimmerer
- Market economies are built upon hoarding resources to create artificial scarcities
	- Rewards greed while also decreasing the quality of life for the average citizen
	- Drastically increased total consumption in comparison to gift economies
	- Metaphor with the hunter: If every hunter needed their own boar, the population would collapse
- In another sense, the economy is connected to the environment also because economies exist within the environment
	- The model gift economy using the serviceberry and birds is an example. It proves to us that gift based economies do not just work, but are the dominant economy in nature
- The ultimate goal of gift economies is the mutual flourishing of the community as a whole. Contrasts with market capitalism, which is focused on the individual
	- Swaps out the trade of depleting material wealth with gratitude, an infinitely replenishing resource
	- Encourage the use or re-use of products that otherwise would have gone to waste

Victor
- Victor explains how the current trajectory of growth based economies are not viable for the long-term stability of the environment
	- So long as growth is of primary importance, environmental stability will always be of concern. Even if growth is decoupled from resource inputs, the rebound effect ensures that the net resource consumption will increase
- Growth based economies clearly do not contribute to the total quality of life, considering the deviation between the genuine progress indicator and GDP
- Methods of degrowth, like a shorter work year, can increase the quality of work, while also spreading employment opportunities to more people
- Questions if capitalism could survive through a degrowth
	- There is little reason to see it completely die, because some sectors of the economy would surge (like renewable energy development) in a degrowth economy
- In summary, degrowth is a method we can use to promote long-lasting environmentally resilient civilizations while also improving our quality of life

Appolloni
- American culture and the imposed psychological need to consume
- Market capitalism requires unstable consumption to thrive, perhaps even to exist. Capitalist dominated cultures are therefore strategically manipulated by the biggest corporations to consume as much as possible
	- This sort of mindset is intrinsically built into the capitalist desire for growth
- Based on his work regarding psychological resilience, Appolloni may argue that one throughway may be to tackle this mindset in the same ways we tackle eco-anxiety
	- Teach people to spot the signs of over consumption, and the commonly employed strategies used to encourage impulse purchasing (increasing waste generation)
	- This kind of education helps to build awareness and hope, but also shows people the ways they can help the environmental movement. Starting small with reducing consumption and waste.

# Analyze
- I agree with the prospects of degrowth. Without degrowth our consumption will continue to spiral out of control
- Like Raj Patel and Jason Moore, I also believe that there is good reason to claim capitalism is a root part of the problem
	- According to Patel and Moore, capitalism has for hundreds of years been built on its ability to transform nature into something profitable
	- Although not a primary concern in their written work, I believe the authors here would agree part of the blame lies in the capitalist framework
- In contradiction to the previous point, I also agree with Kimmerer when she points out that market capitalism is unlikely to change any time soon
	- The discussions we have about gift economies and other similar adjustment to capitalism are important because they represent alternative transition stages we might have as we move away from capitalism
	- As already established, the root of capitalism is unsustainable. The framework demands consumption.
	- However, like energy generation, it can help to consider alternative sources we can rely on prior to swapping to a fully renewable society
	- Due to geological limitations, renewable resources can struggle to deliver enough power to modern communities. But keeping coal and oil plants active is also not a good choice. Nuclear, therefore, has become a common midpoint option for countries still trying to find reliable renewable options
