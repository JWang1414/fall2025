Leopold
- "How Wolves Change Rivers" applies numerous of the strategies Leopold had recognized in the 20th century
	- Leopold recognized humans as a part of the land community, and so have a role to play in ecological conservation
	- Leopold grounded his ethics in the health of entire ecosystems, anticipating modern conservation biology's focus on biodiversity
- Trophic cascades and wolves in Yellowstone is an exact replica of Leopold's personal experiences.
	- The deep complexity and series of events after the reintroduction of wolves to the park justifies Leopold's claim that humans cannot claim themselves as the conquerors of nature. The biosphere is too vast, manipulating it for the exclusively the benefit of humans is unlikely to be conducive for long-term resilience
