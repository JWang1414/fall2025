A conversation among one of the contributors to the document, “World Scientists’ Warning to Humanity: A Second Notice,” Leah Thomas, Rutger Bregman and you.

Topic:
- Each of them bring forth data driven statistics regarding where we have fallen short in developing our societies
	- Bregman uses repeated examples of people working together in disaster
	- Scientists use numbers and statistics to prove how the downfall of the Earth has progressed since the last 25 years
	- Thomas points out, based on studies and statistics, that environmental injustices disproportionately affect people of colour
- Suggest that we must acknowledge inequality, believe in human capacity, and act on evidence
- Connecting the pieces placed by Thomas, Bregman, and the world scientists, they are essentially pulling together the frameworks for a movement and society that thrives on cooperation
	- Cooperation is more effective than the cynical power structures that permeate modern civilization
	- They make sure not just to prove their suggests may be successful, but show us that this approach has worked in the past
# Convey
What do these authors say about the environment's current state _and_ what do they each suggest solutions to be? Include your own opinion about these author's different views and how they come together to understand the current state of the environment and ways forward

Leah Thomas, 2020
- Connects air quality issues, the COVID-19 pandemic, and police violence
- Shows how environmental and social injustices compound each other in communities of colour
- Environmental injustices disproportionately affect Black, Brown, and Indigenous communities
	- Pollution concentrated in inner cities and rural communities of colour
- Concerns about Flint's water crisis and food deserts were often overlooked. They are not seen as central issues
- *Intersectional environmentalism*: addressing both planetary degradation and the mistreatment of people, recognizing that people of colour face the harshest climate impacts currently
- If the environmental movement fights to protect ecosystems while ignoring communities being poisoned by pollution, it undermines its own moral authority
	- Thomas recognizes climate solutions must address existing inequities, lest they perpetuate harm to the most vulnerable populations

Rutger Bregman, 2020
- Belief is a self-fulfilling prophecy. That is, our distrust of human nature functions similarly to a worldwide nocebo effect
	- We create systems and behaviours that bring out those traits
- Extensive research on catastrophes (Titanic, 9/11, Hurricane Katrina) reveals "veneer" theory is empirically false
	- Our perception of reality is propagated through the media. Our own negativity bias builds "mean world syndrome"
- *The myth of homo economicus*: Economic theory built on the assumption of selfish, calculating humans doesn't reflect reality
- A pessimistic view of humanity justifies top-down control and management, while recognizing human decency implies we need different, more collaborative leadership models
	- There is reason to believe a significant pushback against human decency is because it would inconvenience the most powerful
- Applied to the environmental crisis, Bregman is suggesting that such a collective problem requires extensive cooperation.
	- We should begin to work towards building a system based on collaboration and shared values, as opposed to coercion and punishment
	- The environmental movement can harness this inherent cooperativeness rather than assuming people need to be forced into sustainable behaviour
	- Moreover, the "mean world syndrome" he describes may be a significant barrier to climate action

Warning to Humanity, 2017
- Humanity has failed to make sufficient progress on environmental challenges, 25 years after the original warning in 1992
- Emission and dead zones have increased, while vertebrate abundance declined. The human population and ruminant livestock have also increased, placing more stress on ecosystems
- Material consumption is "geographically and demographically uneven," pointing to environmental injustice where impacts are not equally distributed
- Outline 13 specific steps
	- "soon it will be too late to shift course away from our failing trajectory."
	- Emphasize that there may be a point of no return
- Cite ozone layer recovery, poverty reduction, declining fertility rates from girls' education, and renewable energy growth as proof that decisive action works, and is possible
# Analyze
- Connection to Simon Appolloni's (2022, 2023) writing on psychological resilience
	- Each of them call for widespread cooperation. They highlight where we have gone wrong with statistics and facts, and yet also spend the time to explain how we have improved, and show that we have already done so in the past
	- The idea of hope as something that pushes people into action
	- Moving beyond fear-based messaging to solutions-oriented approaches that activate our natural inclination toward cooperation during crisis
	- Much of the 13 steps provided by the scientists are individually applicable. Personal choices (dietary shifts), or community engagement
- Silent spring is another example of how humans have moved past environmental crises before
- The scientists acknowledge the intersection between environmental problems and environmental justice.
	- Call to "reduce wealth inequality" and ensure "social justice around the world"
- The scientists validate Bregman's thesis by noting that progress happens through "civil-society pressure," "grassroots efforts," and collective action
	- The document, signed by 15 000 scientists, in a sense demonstrates Bregman's principle in collective action
- Both Thomas and Bregman point toward a similar insight: inclusive, collaborative approaches work better than top-down control or cynical assumptions about human nature
	- Assume people are willing to cooperate
- The scientists demonstrate Bregman's point while warning about our failures
	- Humans _can_ cooperate effectively, but we're just not doing it sufficiently yet
	- The Dragons of Inaction (2011) may have a place to play in our current inefficiency
