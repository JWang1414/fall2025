### Primates in Urban Landscapes
Mammals and non-human primates have a long history living with humans and in human environments. Something like 2000 years. Numerous cultures have close ties with them, such as the construction of monkey temples.

Historically, despite having spent such a long time living in human environments, often times they are left to take the blame. For example, during an incident when monkeys had run out to search for food because of a lack of tourism, they were the ones forced into enclosures.

These monkeys had been adapted into urban environments over the course of thousands of years, and now that we cannot provide for them, we place the blame on them.

Increased urbanisation and tourism have lead to primates from forests into human-dominated landscapes. Tourist presence specifically has resulted in behavioural and physiological changes, increased disease risk, and domestic animal predation.

75-77% of terrestrial ecosystems have been modified by humans, typically for land use such as developing pastures, plantations, or creating human settlements. This forces animals into smaller habitats, and the modified, localised habitat changes worse at carrying the same animals.

Ultimately, we cannot stop human-wildlife coexistence, and so we require methods to reduce human-wildlife "conflict" and promote coexistence.

One strong solution is to promote education and understanding of people's surrounding wildlife.
