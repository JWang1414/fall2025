### Green Growth
Growth, economic growth, has now been baked into the systems of our society, and so is now essential for it to continue to exist.

But can we decouple the resource input and pollution? This would require designing the economic processes so that the economic output is less dependent on material throughput
- Making the same amount of money, but using less resources?

Lets say we accomplish this. We get more for less, what happens? Well, the *rebound effect* begins to take place. That is, since people can purchase more, they will buy more, and therefore offset the benefits.

Ultimately, you cannot reach sustainability with growth, because the reductions are voided by the bolstered economy.
### Ecological Economics
- This is the real one

Stopping growth with no plan doesn't work. In practice it ends up increasing unemployment, poverty, and debt. Emissions go down, but it's not really worth it.

The principles of a steady-state economy:
- The harvest of renewable resources should not exceed their regeneration rate
- the rate of extraction of non-renewable resources should not exceed the rate of creation of renewable substitutes;
- and waste emissions should not exceed the environment's capacity to assimilate them.
- add the protection of land and water to reduce competition among humans and other species.
- A key ingredient is a shorter work year, which would help to spread employment among more of the labour force
- better-funded anti-poverty programmes, a stable population

Generally speaking, we would like to choose the right things to grow. Ideally, we invest into regions such as literacy, education, social justice, environmental quality etc.
- In the modern day we of course have very efficient growth such as material and energy throughput, degradation of air, and stocks of physical capital

The idea of the Genuine Progress Indicator is one that measures progress in terms of education, crime, long-term environmental damage, the lifespan of consumer durables etc. Hilariously, its trends appear entirely disconnected from the growth in GDP
