Part of the significance of Gaia theory is the change in mindset one has to adopt upon accepting it. Because everything is interconnected, the environmental movement is not just protecting something external, but they are protecting themselves.
### Land Ethic
Aldo Leopold, a man from 1887-1948, proposed the idea of "thinking like a mountain". That is understanding the dynamics of life from a member of the biotic community.

"Just as a deer herd lives in mortal fear of its wolves, so does a mountain live in mortal fear of its deer"

- Related to the deep inter-connectivity of the ecosystem. We cannot remove one part without expecting a change elsewhere.
- To be a conqueror implies that one must also know what "makes the community clock tick". For a conqueror to be successful they must understand the role and value of everything in a system
	- Humans, clearly, do not, and so we cannot assume the role of conquerors in Gaia

> "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability, and beauty of the biotic community. It is wrong when it tends otherwise."
- This is the exact statement of the land ethic

Although Leopold believed that education and policies could be helpful, he believed that, with current method, they would not be the most useful. In order for people to foster an obligation towards the Earth, we also need to instil "love, respect, and admiration" for the land.

*Trophic Cascade*: The consequences that occur when a top predator is hunted or replenished in a system.
- Manifest as a series of population changes in numerous organisms, plants included
### Deep Ecology
One way to think of it is anything that isn't "shallow ecology". The stuff that helps, but isn't really that helpful. It consists of policies that do not change the paradigm
- Recycling
- Hybrid cars

Principles:
1. The well-being and flourishing of human and nonhuman life on Earth have value in themselves (synonyms: intrinsic value, inherent value). These values are independent of the usefulness of the nonhuman world for human purposes.
	- All things, even the ones that humans deem as "invaluable" have innate value associated with them
2. Richness and diversity of life forms contribute to the realisation of these values and are also values in themselves.
	- Bio-diverse communities tend to be more resilient
3. Humans have no right to reduce this richness and diversity except to satisfy vital human needs
There are a few more principles, but they have not been highlighted by our prof.

### Soil
"The upper layer of Earth in which plants grow ... consists of a mixture of organic remains, living animals, and clay, and rock particles"
- Source of 95% of our food
- Carbon sink
- Current farming practices destroy soil. It releases the carbon, and annihilates the built up resources.

However, we are currently developing methods to farm without destroying soil, such as no-till farming.
