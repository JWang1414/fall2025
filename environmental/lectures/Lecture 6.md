### Veneer Theory
Humans as a whole generally have a more grim view of ourselves than reality.

*Lord of the Flies* by William Golding claims that, after being placed on a desert island, children and people are bound to revert to their savage selves. However, we find that this doesn't reflect reality. It is logically backed that, in times of desperation, humans are more likely to help each other.

Hurricane Katrina in New Orleans is another example of this.
### Nocebo
What we believe can be real. If you believe coke makes you sick, or if you believe a pill will cure your sickness, it can become reality. People can experience real results from fabricated beliefs.

The takeaway is that, if we choose to believe something, we often find evidence to sustain those beliefs.
- The idea of the self-fulfilling prophecy

So, how can we apply this to humans? Well, just because we believe the world is worse than it is, not only do tragedies stand out to us, but we may even inadvertently prop up the negativity we already hold.
### The Media
The news is far more likely to report on dramatisations. Exciting news stories and tragedies. This is what sells, and so this is what is promoted.

And so, seeing the news, this belief is implanted into society.
- Also mentioned in the readings is that the media is more likely to be inaccurate than accurate
### Looking Forward from the Past
In 1973, there was a series of tragedies and grim predictions worldwide. The economy was doing worse, the Cold War was going on, and scientists were predicting a population bomb in the global south.
- The population prediction was incorrect. There was a pervasive and racist belief that high birth rates were the primary issue. But it turns out it was skewed by mass consumption in the west
