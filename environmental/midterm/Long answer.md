Convey:
- Capitalism thrives because it could turn nature into something productive, and transform that productivity into wealth
- Defined by conquest. The idea that nature and society are unique and separated from each other made plunder a way of life
- The final goal of capitalism is to transform nature into something that is marketable
	- For example, crops. Mono-cultures are less effective than diversified mixes, but much harder to monetise. A lord could not advertise consistent crop yields, and broker deals if the yield is unreliable
	- Mono-cultures thrived because they made more money. Feudal lords prioritised short term growth over sustainability
- Conquest is profitable. Trading the surplus of resources one might find in a foreign country makes money
	- Furthermore, colonialists could define the people they found there as "nature" and therefore separate from society. Under this impression, colonialists would gladly enslave native peoples
	- Rene Descartes' "thinking things" and "extended things" model. He asserted that European civilisation must become the "masters and possessors of nature"
Analysis
- It follows from the heavy implication that capitalism is largely responsible for climate disaster that the issues the environmental movement must face are rooted deeply within society
- Capitalism and its ravenous need to profit is in no small part responsible for the modern abuse of nature and minority groups
- The history of European dominance over native peoples, and their classification of minorities as "natural" has inevitably resulted in unequal ecologies within our society (Stephan Scharper)
	- Minorities are often disadvantaged and worse off than their cishet white counterparts as a consequence of past oppression. This fact continues into modern life, the poor remain easier to "pick on", and so environmental tragedy is more often to strike within poorer communities
- Also probably possible to make a connection with intersectionality within the environmental movement (Leah Thomas) but the points would be roughly similar to what I just described

	- The success of capitalism is owed to empowering the greediest and most selfish. We are so convinced that humans are selfish because the most powerful tell us that it is so
	- Capitalism has not just resulted in the disrespect of nature, but also fractures our society in a way that helps to maintain the status quo
